WEIGHT CHART

Cuban link chain weight chart.

Estimated weights for solid Cuban link chains by width, length, and karat — checked against the weights manufacturers themselves publish.

Two kinds of numbers appear in this chart, and they are never blended. The gold figure in every cell is an Estimate from our weight model. Where a maker prints a weight for that exact size, the cell also carries a green Published figure with its source one tap away. Where no maker publishes a weight, you see the estimate alone — labeled as what it is.

This chart covers solid chains only. No manufacturer publishes per-size weights for hollow Cuban links, so hollow chains are not charted: a hollow chain weighs far less than a solid chain of the same dimensions, and the honest move is to say so rather than guess. If you already have a chain, a scale beats any chart — weigh it, then run the number through the Cuban link calculator.

How accurate is the estimate?

On October 31, 2026, we checked the model behind this chart against 77 manufacturer-published weights from 8 makers — spec tables, catalog listings, and individual product pages, each traced to the page it came from. The median published weight sits within about 2% of the model (+1.6%), 83% of the 77 land within ±10%, and all but three land within ±20%. For solid chains in the 5–12mm heart of the chart, the estimate is a well-corroborated central figure — which is why the rest of the site presents modeled weights as a range, not a promise.

The check also found the model's known drift, and this chart does not hide it. Published 10K weights run about 7% above the model. Chains at 4mm and under run heavier than the model — the clasp effect again — while at 14mm and over real chains come in lighter than width-squared scaling predicts. Published 18K weights rest almost entirely on one maker, and 22K on one maker alone. Two figures published in one maker's prose (190 g and 366 g) contradicted that maker's own spec tables and were excluded from the check entirely.

The full method — the 0.077 formula, the alloy densities, and the original 265 g calibration anchor this model was tuned to — is published on the methodology page, including a section on this validation.
