ABOUT THIS SITE

Know your gold. Know your price.

Cuban Link Calculator is a free educational tool that shows you what the gold in a chain is worth — before a store adds its premium or a buyer subtracts its margin — so you can walk into any gold transaction already knowing the number that matters.

Why this site exists

Most gold jewelry is bought and sold in the dark. A buyer is handed a price with no breakdown of how much is metal and how much is markup. A seller is handed an offer with no explanation of how it was calculated. In both cases, one side of the transaction knows exactly what the gold is worth and the other side is guessing. This site exists to close that gap. Gold is a commodity with a public market price, and the value of the gold in a chain is simple arithmetic. Once that arithmetic is in the buyer's and seller's hands, negotiations become what they should be: a discussion about workmanship, service, and margin, conducted in the open.

How the calculator works

The live spot price

When the calculator loads, it fetches the current spot price of gold — US dollars per troy ounce — from the gold-api.com market feed. Every value on the page is computed from that live price in your browser. The price field is editable, so you can substitute any reference price you trust, and if the feed is unavailable the site says so and labels the displayed price rather than presenting stale data as live.

The purity model

Values are computed from purity: nominal karat fractions (14K is 14/24, about 58.33%) or an exact fineness you enter from a hallmark stamp, such as 585 for 58.5%. Weight can be entered in grams, pennyweight, troy ounces, or tola, and non-gold weight — stones, steel springs, clasps — can be subtracted so it is never valued as gold.

The Cuban link weight estimator

For buyers shopping by size, the build estimator models a solid Miami Cuban link from length and width, adjusting for the density of each karat's alloy. It is calibrated against published handmade maker specifications; a solid 14K chain at 12mm wide and 24 inches long, for example, comes out at roughly 265 grams. Because real makers vary, the estimate is always presented as a range (about −12% to +20%), never as a false-precision exact weight. A measured weight from a scale beats the model every time, and the site says so wherever the estimate appears.

The premium and payout layers

On top of melt value, the calculator offers two clearly labeled scenarios. The buying view adds a premium percentage that you choose, or analyzes a real quote you paste in, and reports the premium in dollars, as a percentage, and as a price per gram. The selling view models a buyer's percentage of melt minus fees. Neither view is a listing, an offer, or a prediction — they are transparent arithmetic on numbers you supply.

Who it is for

The site is written for first-time Cuban link buyers comparing quotes, for owners checking what their chain is worth before selling or pawning it, and for anyone who owns gold — chains, bracelets, rings, coins, or scrap — and wants its metal value at today's price. The accompanying guides explain how Cuban link pricing works, how karats and hallmarks work, and what melt value means when you sell.

Accuracy limits, stated honestly

  • Estimates are estimates. Dimension-based weights are modeled ranges. Real chains vary by maker, profile, and clasp; weigh the actual piece for a real valuation.
  • A stamp is not a test. The calculator values gold at the purity you enter. It cannot authenticate a piece or verify that a hallmark is truthful — only physical testing can do that.
  • Prices move. Gold trades around the clock, and a value computed this morning is not this afternoon's value. Re-run the calculation on the day you transact.
  • Scenarios are not quotes. Premium and payout examples are illustrative. This site has no jeweler affiliations and no sponsored listings, sells nothing, and buys nothing.
  • Education, not financial advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell anything. For significant transactions, confirm weight, fineness, and terms with the business you are dealing with.
