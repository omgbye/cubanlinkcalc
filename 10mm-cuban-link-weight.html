WEIGHT CHART · 10MM

10mm Cuban link chain weight.

What a solid 10mm Cuban link weighs at every standard length and karat — estimates checked against the weights manufacturers publish for this exact width, and what that gold is worth.

The short answer: a solid 10mm Cuban link in 14K at the most common length, 24 inches, weighs about 185 grams. That is our model's estimate — and for this exact size, makers publish their own figures: GOLDZENN prints 185 g and TraxNYC's listings median works out to 199 g. At 18 inches the same chain estimates at 139 g; at 30 inches, 231 g. Karat moves the number too, because denser alloys weigh more at the same dimensions.

Two kinds of numbers appear on this page, and they are never blended. Table figures are Estimates from our weight model. Where a maker prints a weight for an exact 10mm size, it appears separately as a green Published figure with its source one tap away. Everything here covers solid chains only — no manufacturer publishes per-size weights for hollow Cuban links, so a hollow 10mm weighs far less than any figure on this page, and the honest move is to say so rather than guess. If you already have the chain, a scale beats any chart: weigh it, then run the number through the Cuban link calculator.

How the published weights grade the estimate at 10mm

At this width, the published evidence is unusually direct. GOLDZENN's spec table puts a solid 14K 10mm × 24 in at 185 g — exactly the model's figure. TraxNYC, working independently from medians of its own solid listings, lands at 199 g, about 8% above the model. In 18K, GOLDZENN publishes 225 g and a TraxNYC product lists 230 g, both roughly 5–8% above the model's 214 g. GOLDZENN's 22K ladder tracks the model within about 1–2% at every length — though that is one maker's figures, not independent confirmation. A seller-published 14K listing (Jewelry Affairs) prints 227 g at 26 in and 248 g at 30 in, about 13% and 7% above the model.

The pattern at 10mm is the pattern across the whole chart: published solid weights cluster at or slightly above the estimate, which is why the site presents modeled weights as a range rather than a promise. The full width-by-width evidence is on the Cuban link weight chart, and the method behind the model is published on the methodology page.

What a 10mm Cuban link is worth

Worth starts with melt value: the value of the gold content alone, at the market price of gold. The math is three numbers multiplied together — weight in grams, purity, and the spot price of pure gold per gram (the spot price per troy ounce divided by 31.1035). Craftsmanship, brand, and condition sit on top of melt, not inside it: a store's price is melt plus a premium, and a scrap buyer's offer is melt minus a deduction.

The numbers below are an illustration at a sample spot price of $3,876.40 per troy ounce, not today's price — the calculator runs the same math on the live price from the Live Market Feed. At that sample price, pure gold works out to about $124.63 per gram, so a 14K gram (58.3% pure) is worth about $72.70. The solid 14K 10mm × 24 in chain from the table — 185 g estimated — carries a melt value of roughly $13,450. Across the model's weight range for that size (163 g to 222 g), melt runs from roughly $11,800 to $16,100 at the same sample price.

What you would actually pay, or be paid, is a different number. Retail adds a premium over melt for the making of the chain — measuring that premium is how you compare quotes honestly. Selling runs the other way: buyers offer a percentage of melt, and the percentage is the part you can negotiate. Either direction starts from the same place: know the weight, know the melt.

10mm Cuban link questions

How much does a 10mm Cuban link chain weigh?

A solid 10mm Cuban link in 14K weighs an estimated 139 g at 18 inches, 185 g at 24 inches, and 231 g at 30 inches. Those are model estimates with a −12% to +20% range; for the most common size, 14K × 24 in, manufacturers publish 185 g (GOLDZENN) and 199 g (TraxNYC) for that exact chain. Karat changes the weight at the same dimensions — a solid 10mm × 24 in estimates at 160 g in 10K and 214 g in 18K — and a hollow 10mm weighs far less than any of these figures.

How much is a 10mm Cuban link chain worth?

Its floor is its melt value: weight × purity × the spot price of pure gold per gram. As a worked example at a sample spot price of $3,876.40 per troy ounce — not today's price — a solid 14K 10mm × 24 in chain at the estimated 185 g melts at roughly $13,450. A store will charge melt plus a premium for making the chain; a scrap buyer will offer a percentage of melt. For the live figure, run the weight and karat through the Cuban link calculator at today's price.

Why does my 10mm chain weigh less than the chart?

The most likely reason is construction: these figures are for solid chains, and a hollow or semi-hollow 10mm can weigh half as much while looking the same size. Link profile matters too — flatter or more widely spaced links carry less metal at the same stated width. No manufacturer publishes per-size weights for hollow Cuban links, so there is no honest hollow chart to compare against. Weigh the chain and enter the measured weight in the calculator; melt value follows the metal that is actually there.
