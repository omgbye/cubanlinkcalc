THE COMPARISON

What's different

    How we calculated this

    Link copied. It carries these quote inputs — and your manual spot price, if you set one. A live spot price is never frozen into a link; the page fetches it fresh when the link opens.

    Construction matters to every comparison here: at the same outside dimensions, a hollow chain weighs less than a solid one, and durability varies with alloy and construction — not karat alone. This page compares weight and price math only; it does not authenticate jewelry, and a premium can include labor, clasp design, finishing, overhead and brand as well as profit. Compare pre-tax quotes on the same basis, and confirm weight and fineness with each seller.

    A worked example

    Example spot price: $4,000 per troy ounce — an example price chosen for clean arithmetic, the same illustration used on the Methodology page. It is not a current market price. At that spot, pure gold works out to $4,000 ÷ 31.1035 ≈ $128.60 per gram. Two quotes for solid 14K chains:

    • Example A — 100 g, quoted at $9,000. Price per gram: $9,000 ÷ 100 g = $90.00. Melt value: 100 g × 0.5833 (14K purity, 14/24) × $128.60 ≈ $7,501.84. Premium over melt: $9,000 − $7,501.84 = $1,498.16, which is 20.0% over melt — $14.98 of premium in each gram.
    • Example B — 90 g, quoted at $8,600. Price per gram: $8,600 ÷ 90 g ≈ $95.56. Melt value: 90 g × 0.5833 × $128.60 ≈ $6,751.66. Premium over melt: $8,600 − $6,751.66 = $1,848.34, which is 27.4% over melt — $20.54 of premium in each gram.

    What differs between them, stated plainly: Quote B's total is $400 lower, and it also lists 10 g less chain — the totals and the weights move together, so the per-gram figures are what separate them: $90.00 against $95.56. Measured against the gold itself, Quote A's price sits 20.0% above melt and Quote B's 27.4% — a gap of $350.18 in premium dollars. And neither total here includes tax or shipping; if one seller's quote folds those in and the other's does not, the comparison shifts by exactly that amount.     Comparing quotes fairly

    • Same karat? Compare price per gram. Between two chains of the same karat, the per-gram price lines up cleanly, because every gram carries the same amount of gold.
    • Different karats? Compare premium over melt. A gram of 18K holds more gold than a gram of 14K, so its per-gram price starts higher before any premium is added. The premium percentage measures what each seller adds on top of the gold value, whatever the karat — that is the like-for-like number across karats.
    • Construction changes the weight behind the dimensions. A hollow chain can look identical to a solid one and weigh far less, and gold value follows weight, not silhouette. If a quote gives dimensions but no weight, ask for the weight; any size-based estimate — including the one in this tool — is an estimate with a range, not a measurement.
    • Ask what the total includes. Tax, shipping and fees change the amount you actually pay, and this comparison is only as honest as the totals entered. Enter like-for-like totals: pre-tax, with the same extras in or out on every quote.

    One more thing the tool does for you: every quote on this page is worked against a single spot price at a single moment, with the source and timestamp shown. Sellers quote at different times against a moving market; running their totals through one price is what makes the premiums comparable. Selling instead of buying?
